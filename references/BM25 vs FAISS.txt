  In simple terms, BM25 and FAISS are two completely different ways of searching documents. We combine them ("Hybrid Search") because neither one is
  smart enough to handle financial reports on its own.
  ──────
  ### 1. BM25: The "Exact Keyword Matcher" (Lexical Search)

  • What it is: An advanced, highly optimized keyword-matching algorithm (an improved version of TF-IDF used in search engines).
  • How it works: It looks for the exact words and numbers you typed into the search bar, scoring pages higher if those rare words appear frequently.
  • Superpower: Perfect for exact financial codes, ticker symbols, and accounting jargon.
      • Example: If someone searches "ASC 842", "Item 1A", "TSLA", or "2023", BM25 finds the exact page in milliseconds (< 15 ms).
  • Weakness: It has zero understanding of meaning or synonyms.
      • If the user asks "money spent on factories", BM25 will fail to find the section titled "Capital Expenditures" because none of the exact words
      match.

  ──────
  ### 2. FAISS: The "Meaning & Concept Matcher" (Vector Search)

  • What it is: Facebook AI Similarity Search — an open-source library built by Meta (Facebook).
  • How it works: An embedding model converts your question and all PDF chunks into arrays of numbers (vectors). FAISS then plots them in multi-
  dimensional space and finds the chunks closest in meaning.
  • Superpower: Understands concepts, context, and synonyms.
      • Example: If someone searches "cash burn rate", FAISS easily finds "free cash flow deficit" or "operational liquidity decline".
  • Weakness: It is terrible at distinguishing subtle numbers or exact codes.
      • To an embedding model, the number "2022" and "2023" look virtually identical because they appear in the exact same sentence contexts!
